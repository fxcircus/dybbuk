# The wildness pass — staged plan

Roy, 2026-09-07: "everything works technically but this plugin is meant to be
much more experimental than it currently is ... you can't really push it very
far and get musical results."

This file is the plan that came out of that. It was produced by a multi-agent
round: eight independent lenses audited the build for ceilings and a second
round adversarially verified every claim (several were refuted and are recorded
as such), then four independent designs were written from different
philosophies, scored head to head by three judge lenses, and synthesised.

**Stage 1 is done** and is written up in `docs/PROGRESS.md`. Stages 2 to 4 are
below as specified, with the four calls that belong to Roy listed at the end.
Anything here can still be argued with — the measurements are the part to
trust, not the prescriptions.

Where a stage-1 number below disagrees with PROGRESS, PROGRESS wins: two of the
plan's prescriptions were measured and changed during implementation (the tap
weights, and kDecayMax's justification).

########## STAGE 2: The chaos gets its own hand, and the clock gets room
GOAL: Split the one macro into per-source gains so the chaotic source has a control of its own, wire the cells that already have live consumers, give Agitation a per-sample route to the clock so a rate knob finally bends pitch, unhook the chip's destruction from the Time knob, and put four faces on the plate. Two new parameters, four new knobs.

  [constant] Source/dsp/ChipConstants.h :: kFsChipHardMax / kFsChipHardMin
    FROM: 250000.0f / 750.0f: log2(250000/200000) = 0.322 octaves of upward room at Time 0 and log2(1500/750) = 1.000 octave down at Time 1, against a modulation clamp of 2.0 octaves. Deep FM squares off against a hard wall at both ends of the Time knob, which is a click.
    TO:   600000.0f / 500.0f, stated and enforced as an INVARIANT: min(headroom up at Time 0, headroom down at Time 1) = log2(3) = 1.585 oct must strictly exceed kTimeModClampOct, which becomes 1.5. The modulator is therefore compressed by its own clamp before the clock is ever flat-topped, at EVERY Time setting.
    WHY:  The only formulation of this fix whose deep-FM behaviour is identical at Time 0 and Time 1; the other three proposals all widened the clamps and then set kTimeModClampOct larger than the headroom they had just bought. Update ChipClock.h:21's '(0 to 6)' comment to '(0 to about 14)'. Cost is CPU, not correctness: worst case rises from 4.2 to 12.5 chip ticks per host sample at 48 kHz against a measured 0.25 % of realtime, and PTStage's write loop is unbounded so nothing overruns.

  [bugfix] Source/dsp/ChipClock.cpp :: the bleed exponent, line 68
    FROM: const float x = std::log (kBleedOnsetHz / fs) * kInvLogBleed; — no clamp. kInvLogBleed normalises against 1500 Hz, so below that x exceeds 1 and bleedLvl rises past its calibrated maximum: 1.43x at 750 Hz, 2.07x at 500 Hz.
    TO:   const float x = juce::jmin (1.0f, std::log (pt::kBleedOnsetHz / fs) * pt::kInvLogBleed);
    WHY:  NON-NEGOTIABLE, and it must land in the SAME COMMIT as kFsChipHardMin above. Unclamped, widening the clock quietly re-opens the pitched clock squeal the user removed by ear one commit ago — PROGRESS identifies it as a 2.75 kHz tone at a 1 s delay, and fs/2 at 1 s is 2751 Hz, an exact match. One of the four proposals diagnosed this bug in its own NOT-DOING section and then committed it two sections later.

  [constant] Source/dsp/ModConstants.h :: kTimeModMaxOct / kTimeModClampOct, new inline timeModOctaves()
    FROM: kTimeModMaxOct 0.25f applied LINEARLY at two sites that can drift apart; kTimeModClampOct 2.0f. Peak deviation through the shipped path is 0.230 oct, 2.8 semitones, flat across the whole knob. The comment records that 2.0 octaves on a LINEAR knob was unusable past an eighth of the travel — a taper problem answered by an 8x amputation, when docs/design/inputs/tests.md had already specified a square taper.
    TO:   kTimeModMaxOct 1.25f, kTimeModClampOct 1.5f, and ONE helper used by both sites and by the UI ring: `inline float timeModOctaves (float t) noexcept { return kTimeModMaxOct * (0.2f * t + 0.8f * t * t * t * t); }` — algebraically 0.25·t + 1.0·t⁴. Depth by knob position: 0.0328 oct at 13 % (today 0.0325), 0.0831 at 30 %, 0.1875 at 50 %, 0.3325 at 70 %, 1.25 oct (15 semitones) at 100 %.
    WHY:  This is the taper the user actually asked for, not a reversal of their decision: the curve is EXACTLY today's linear curve plus a quartic tail, so every position they tuned by ear is unchanged to within 1 %, and the destroyed-pitch region that never existed is the top 30 %. One helper means the two application sites and the ring can never disagree. 1.25 < 1.5 means Time Mod alone never clamps; stacked with Chaos it does, which is the clamp doing its job inside the clock's 1.585 oct of headroom.

  [wiring] Source/dsp/ModMatrix.cpp :: prepare(), the depth table (lines 40-42)
    FROM: Three of 28 cells non-zero. depth[*][dstResonance], [dstAbsorb], [dstBlend] and [dstStrength] are provably zero for the life of the plugin — setDepth has no caller anywhere — so kScaleResonance, kScaleAbsorb, kScaleBlend and kScaleStrength multiply zero and four one-pole smoothers run every tick to produce guaranteed zeros.
    TO:   Keep the three existing; add depth[srcAgitation][dstTime] = 0.35f (±0.7 oct at Agitate 100, per sample, bipolar-centred); depth[srcFollower][dstStrength] = 0.45f (±9 dB of input drive); depth[srcFollower][dstFilter] = 0.40f (+1.6 oct on a hit); depth[srcInterference][dstFilter] = 0.35f (±1.4 oct of chaotic cutoff); depth[srcInterference][dstResonance] = 0.35f (±0.175); depth[srcInterference][dstAbsorb] = 0.30f (±0.15). depth[srcInterference][dstBlend] stays 0 until stage 4.
    WHY:  Every patch currently modulates the same three things in the same direction. Follower→Strength is the cheap and safe half of 'playing harder drives the loop harder': it modulates the INPUT into an existing clipper, ahead of the loop, so it cannot destabilise anything. Interference→Filter turns a self-oscillating loop from a parked sine into a chaotically swept squeal; Interference→Absorb is the loop breathing in and out of the earth (probe measures a fifth of the Absorb knob as worth 39 dB of loop level). Resonance at 0.35 rather than the 0.45/0.50 two proposals wanted: from a knob at 0.80 a peak reaches 0.975, tipping it over intermittently rather than parking it in permanent squeal.

    TO:   `const float agit = agitation.processSample (onset); agitSum += agit;` and pass (2.0f * agit - 1.0f) into timeOctave as the third argument.

########## STAGE 3: The instrument plays itself
GOAL: Close the hardware's own generative loop — the loop's energy moves the oscillator's pitch and level, and the oscillator writes new pitched material into the delay — and give the loop the only two sources of brightness it has ever had. Two new parameters, two new trims, one new preset, and the four existing presets re-auditioned ONCE against a settled loop.

  [new-dsp] Source/dsp/ModMatrix.h / Source/dsp/ModConstants.h / Source/dsp/ModMatrix.cpp :: Dst enum, Offsets, kScaleTonesPitch, kScaleTonesLevel, prepare()

    TO:   Add dstTonesPitch, dstTonesLevel (numDst 9). kScaleTonesPitch = 2.0f octaves, kScaleTonesLevel = 1.0f, kSmoothHz 20 Hz each. depth[srcInterference][dstTonesPitch] = 0.60f (±1.2 oct at Chaos 100); depth[srcInterference][dstTonesLevel] = 0.50f (±0.5).

WHAT BOUNDS THE PEAK, stated honestly rather than the way all four proposals stated it.

The claim every proposal made — "the tanh bounds y at 1.05 and the 10 Hz DC blocker recentres that to +/-1.0, so the mono wet cannot exceed 1.0" — is not quite true. tanh does bound LoopSaturator's PRE-blocker output to (-1.050/drive, +0.950/drive), which at kSatDrive 1.2 is (-0.875, +0.792). But `out = y - x1 + r*y1` is a first-order highpass whose impulse response has l1 norm 2.0, so the true worst-case bound is about 1.75, not 0.875. 0.875 is the STEADY-STATE bound for content well above 10 Hz, which is what the loop actually produces — and the architecture really is bounded, but the number to trust is the measured one, not the algebraic one. That is why `soak` is re-run and quoted FIRST, before kDecayMax, kSatDrive and kSpreadMaxWidth are chosen: they are one decision, and PROGRESS's real figures are mono 0.98 / stereo 1.35, not the 0.84 three of the four proposals worked from.

The bounds in order: softClip on the input, now genuinely asymptotic (knee 0.45, std::tanh) rather than a hard limiter pinned at exactly 1.0 above |x| = 1.6, so the top of Strength keeps doing something while the loop input stays bounded at 1.0. PTCore's own kClipKnee 0.8 quantizer clamp on the chip node, reachable via feedback where node peaks around 2.2. LoopSaturator at kSatDrive 1.2, asymptote 0.875, applied BETWEEN the filter and both the feedback and the wet, so it bounds everything Decay, Resonance, Chaos, Crust and the Touch gesture can do. TptSvf's 1 + g(g+k) > 1e-6 guard, which stays positive for k down to about -1.9 against our worst case of -0.08. And kSvfSatLimit 2.0, which is now a bound on character rather than a bound on level.

THE ONE PATH THE SATURATOR DOES NOT BOUND is stereo Spread: side = 0.5 * spread * kSpreadMaxWidth * (wet - wetDelayed). kSpreadMaxWidth 0.9 -> 0.6 against a mono ceiling that fell from ~0.98 to ~0.875 puts the practical stereo peak near 1.10, against today's recorded 1.35 — better on both counts, which is the claim the soak has to confirm. If it measures above 1.0, take 0.45; do not lower kDecayMax instead. Mono is unaffected by construction: L + R sums back exactly to the wet at any width.

NOT TOUCHED, deliberately: kWetMakeup stays 1.0 (it sits inside the wet path and lifts the hiss by the same amount it lifts the signal — the tap rebalance is the free version), and the Out ceiling stays +6 dB. Two proposals wanted +12 or +18. The level came back from the attenuators, so a bigger fader would only make it easier to clip, and PROGRESS already records Spread costing headroom.


########## REJECTED
CARRIED FORWARD FROM PROPOSALS BUT DROPPED OR FIXED, with the reason.

BURN (destroyer's centrepiece — a level-dependent boost multiplied into the saturator's tanh and deliberately not divided out by invDrive). DROPPED. Two judges found it fatal as written. It does not "accelerate the bloom": it slams the loop to the asymptote and holds it, replacing "always a sine at -8 dBFS" with "always a square at 0 dBFS", which is one timbre at one level — the exact criticism the diagnosis makes of today's runaway. Its "changes nothing about an ordinary echo" is false (boost 1.72, i.e. +4.7 dB of loop gain, at loopEnv 0.3), and it is unsmoothed and refreshed only every 16 samples INSIDE the feedback path, which CLAUDE.md's SmoothedValue rule exists to prevent. The escalation it wanted is delivered instead by Follower->Strength and Touch->Strength, which modulate the INPUT into an existing clipper ahead of the loop and cannot destabilise anything. Its one genuinely excellent idea — decayGate, forcing any escalation term to identity from a knob the player is already holding — is kept as the design rule for every route that can exceed the knob's ceiling.

ENVELOPE-TRACKED kSvfSatLimit (instrument's kSvfSatTrack 8.0 and destroyer's equivalent). DROPPED, and this is the single most important refusal in the plan. A judge simulated both forms and measured them latching to a full-scale square at Resonance 100 with Decay at ZERO (loopEnv 0.998, limit 8.5, peak 1.01), because loopEnv -> stateLimit -> filter ring amplitude -> loopEnv closes entirely inside TimeFilterLoop and bypasses Decay, Absorb and Blend. The bound both proposals cited — "the loop saturator plus TptSvf's coefficient guard" — does not apply: the guard is about the denominator and says nothing about pole location, and the saturator is INSIDE the loop that creates the level, not outside it. Destroyer's version was worse: it stayed latched with Decay pulled to 0.3, which is its entire recovery story. Ship the fixed constant 2.0.

kSvfSatLimit 4.0 (unshackle's staged escalation). DROPPED. A judge measured the post-saturator free ring at limit 2.0 with k = -0.08 already reaching 0.952 at fc 8000. There is nowhere to go.

kResOverdrive 0.30 (destroyer, 10x today). DROPPED for 0.08. A verifier measured a bare SVF at limit 4.0 with only k = -0.03 free-ringing to full scale; -0.30 on top of a raised limit is well past anything anyone has measured.

kDecayMax 1.80 / 2.00 (unshackle, destroyer). DROPPED for 1.45, chosen from a measured equilibrium curve rather than a dB budget. The tanh equilibrium saturates: 1.40 -> 2.00 buys about half a dB of RMS, so those proposals spend a quarter to a half of the Decay knob on a region where the sound barely moves.

LINEAR Decay range 0..1.80 (unshackle). DROPPED for the piecewise range, which keeps every sub-unity knob position bit-identical instead of compressing the most-used part of the knob into its bottom half.

kStrengthCeil 2.5 above softClip (destroyer). DROPPED. A judge showed PTCore.h:31-36 is the same fastTanh construction — knee 0.8, kInvClipSpan 5.0, argument reaching 3 at |x| = 1.4 — so everything above 1.4 out of the new softClip would be HARD clipped straight into the chip's write path at 48 kHz with no oversampling. It moves the hard clipper downstream rather than removing it. The knee-and-std::tanh half is taken; the ceiling half is not.

kFsChipHardMax 800000 with kTimeModClampOct 3.5 (unshackle), and 500000/300 (destroyer). DROPPED for the invariant: 600000/500 gives exactly log2(3) = 1.585 oct of headroom in BOTH directions against a 1.5 oct clamp, at every Time setting. Both rejected versions set the clamp larger than the headroom they had just bought, so deep FM still squares off; destroyer's also omitted the bleed clamp it had diagnosed two sections earlier.

CHAOS ON ONE OF FOUR INTERFERENCE ROUTES (unshackle). FIXED, not dropped: the whole srcInterference row rides Chaos, via per-source gains. A knob whose name describes one quarter of its job is the defect this exercise exists to fix.

CRUST FOLDED INTO ABSORB (instrument). DROPPED for its own parameter. It saves a knob and welds destruction back onto darkening and shortening — so a bit-crushed slapback that is still bright and still long, the exact sound the audit says is unreachable, stays unreachable. It also made Absorb do five jobs in a plan whose whole argument is that one macro doing three jobs is what broke the plugin.

SIX trimSpec FACES WITH A NEW Spec.pad FIELD (instrument) and THE ROUTES STRIP AT y 286..326 (performer). DROPPED. The first puts eight faces across the bottom strip with zero gutter and 3 px between a 62 px face and its box edge, where the index ring and needle arrowhead are drawn; the second overlaps the Lamp (x 398..502, y 324..428) by 2 px while claiming "verified geometry, no overlap". Four midSpec knobs in the two measured 208 px windows, and two trims at y 284..318.

FIVE NEW PARAMETERS (performer: agitshape, warp, haunt, feel, touch). REDUCED TO FOUR TOTAL. The per-source gain model delivers the same decoupling with one knob (Chaos) instead of three, TOUCH's depth becomes the hold rather than a parameter, and three of performer's five were named for internal routing (SHAPE, WARP, HAUNT) rather than for a sound.

ADDING { id::toneslevel } TO BAT CAVE (unshackle, instrument, destroyer). DROPPED — the premise is wrong. tones.advance() and the Time Mod sub term both run regardless of tonesLevel01, so Bat Cave's tonespitch 55 Hz sets its Time Mod FM rate to 27.5 Hz, which is exactly the metallic edge its blurb asks for. Adding a drone would inject a 55 Hz tone into a preset deliberately voiced without one, presented as a bug fix.

kBitsMax/kBitsMin/kBitsCurve AND kSigmaDriveSlope RETUNES (instrument, destroyer). DEFERRED. They change the default grunge of every Time setting on every saved patch, which is a listening pass with its own A/B, and Crust delivers the same destruction on a control the player chooses. Keeping them still is also what makes Crust's orthogonality gate real: Crust 0 must reproduce today's `thd` and `noise` numbers exactly.

OUT CEILING +6 -> +12 or +18 dB (destroyer, performer). DROPPED. The level came back from the attenuators; a bigger fader only makes clipping easier, and PROGRESS already records Spread costing headroom.

AGIT ANGLE / AGIT SHAPE (instrument, performer). DEFERRED. Agitation::setAngle is fully implemented with its anti-click floor and has had zero callers since Phase 3 — genuinely free DSP — but there is no plate slot left, and adding a parameter with no control is precisely the mistake Tones Level, Tones Pitch and Spread already made. It also flattens to a symmetric triangle above about 500 Hz of Agit Speed, which a readout would have to admit. First item in the next canvas revision.

HOST SYNC ON AGIT SPEED (a judge's cross-cutting miss, nobody proposed it). DEFERRED, and named rather than buried. It is a fair criticism — three of four plans make Agit Speed the rate control for tape wow and a metallic FM grid while TimeMap.h already ships a full division table — but it needs a sync toggle on a plate with no room, and it belongs with the Agit Shape pass.

SPREAD ON THE PLATE. DEFERRED. The two windows hold four knobs and Chaos, Crust, Tones and Pitch each unlock something unreachable rather than tuning something reachable.

kEnergyCeilDb AND kIntfWanderScale. NOT TOUCHED. A verifier refuted both: fastTanh is monotone and compressive so the quiet-to-hot amplitude ratio can never exceed the 2.05x input ratio (the whole prize is about 1.4 dB), and raising the energy ceiling was backwards at today's loop level. It may become right once kSatDrive lets loopEnv genuinely reach 0.5 — which is why `soak` now prints loopEnv's 95th percentile. Revisit only if that number says so.

PER-CHIP CLOCK DETUNE. Refuted: the stages are in SERIES so generations 2 and 3 are measurably darker and dirtier (an extra 3.1 and 4.9 dB of cascaded loss beyond what the weights predict), and the runaway is already multi-mode and inharmonic (35 comb peaks within 0.5 dB of the best). It would break the single shared ChipClock::Frame to fix a problem the loop does not have.

FLIPPING kFeedbackFromTapSum. The flag cannot do what its comment says — line 174 picks one node and line 207 sends the same node to the wet, so flipping it deletes the three-step repeat from the OUTPUT and `threestep` fails. The comment is fixed; the flag is not flipped; the tap rebalance takes what the flip was reaching for.

RESTORING THE CLOCK BLEED. The user removed that tone by ear one commit ago, and it is the one finding where the ear call has already been made in the opposite direction. The bleed CLAMP is a safety fix in the other direction, not a step back toward it.

EXTENDING TIME PAST 3.67 s, OVERSAMPLING, A WAVEFOLDER ON THE INPUT PATH, TAKING THE OUTPUT MFB OUT OF THE FEEDBACK PATH, AND THE 28-CELL MATRIX UI. All in IDEAS.md, all for reasons that still hold. The MFB surgery in particular is not even the best lever: a verifier computed that removing it moves the unity band top from 1378 Hz to 1982 Hz, where kDecayMax plus the Colour morph goes further for three lines instead of a topology change.


########## OPEN EAR QUESTIONS
TEN, in the order they will come up.

1. THE ONE I AM LEAST SURE OF: the free-ring level at kSvfSatLimit 2.0 with k = -0.08. A judge's simulation puts it at 0.194 / 0.432 / 0.813 / 0.952 post-saturator at fc 200 / 1000 / 4000 / 8000. If Resonance 100 at Filter 8000 really is a near-full-scale sine with Decay at ZERO, is that "the filter is a voice you can play" or "the knob is a hazard"? The new `resonance` scenario prints it before anything else moves. If it comes back near full scale, kResOverdrive drops to 0.05 BEFORE kSvfSatLimit is touched again — the resonator's amplitude is the one place in this design resting on an extrapolation rather than a printed number.

2. Agitation -> Time makes AGITATE bend pitch as well as sweep the filter, at every existing knob value in every existing patch. Listen at Agitate 15 (Echo-Verb) and 85 (Breathing) before committing; if it is too much, the lever is depth 0.35 -> 0.20, not the route.

3. Bipolar-centring Agitation -> Filter: the downward swallow against the documented hardware normal. This is userDecision 3 and it needs ten seconds of listening, not analysis.

4. kAbsorbDrive 2.0: how gritty should Absorb get, now that it costs 12 dB less level? Held to stage 4 precisely because it is a taste call sitting on top of a saturator whose ceiling it lowers.

5. The Tones wavefolder's alias character at high pitch under +/-1.2 oct of chaotic pitch modulation. At 2093 Hz with fold x8 the folder is broadband against a 48 kHz clock and the chip's 8.8 kHz input filter only removes what is above it, not the products below. The `voice` scenario's alias floor is the gate; if it is ugly rather than characterful, the fix is 2x oversampling of the Tones generator alone, which is cheap because it is one oscillator.

6. Interference -> Blend (stage 4): chaotic dropout and stutter can read as a fault rather than a gesture. It is last and it is cuttable; nothing depends on it.

7. kFollowerFullScale 0.65 is a feel call, not a derivation. Play a guitar and a synth through it at Strength 0 and +12 dB and watch the `follower` print.

8. The Touch column's profile — 20 ms rise, 350 ms fall, a separate 90 ms slew on Time so the pitch swoops while the drive snaps — is a voicing, not a measurement. It is exactly the kind of thing that is right or wrong by ear in ten seconds and cannot be settled by a printed number. Related: should TOUCH get a depth knob after all? It currently has none by design, because a metal contact has no sensitivity control and the hold IS the control.

9. DESIGN.md SECTION 4, ANSWERED AND UNANSWERED. Q1 (feedback topology) is answered but not the way it was asked: the kFeedbackFromTapSum A/B cannot be performed by the code, so the comment is corrected and the tap rebalance takes what the question was reaching for — a shallower comb. Q5 (wet makeup) is answered by the tap rebalance, at zero noise cost, so kWetMakeup stays 1.0 and the question closes. Q6 (Echo-Verb decaying too fast) is answered in stage 3's single re-audition against a settled loop. Q2 (FM law), Q3 (bit and noise calibration) and Q4 (write guard poles) stay open and untouched — Q3 deliberately, so Crust's orthogonality gate stays real.

10. PROGRESS'S OPEN ABSORB FINDING, ANSWERED. "Whether kAbsorbFbMaxDb should come down is an ear question, and it is one constant" — it comes down, 4.0 -> 2.0, against a budget that rises from 0.98 dB to 3.0 dB, so it costs a fifth of the budget instead of four fifths. But the finding's own arithmetic undershoots, because the closed form omits the in-loop absorb shelf inside the feedback path: tune the number against `probe` rows at Absorb 0.0 / 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0, require every row to self-oscillate at Decay 1.45, and rewrite the finding with what was measured rather than deleting it.
